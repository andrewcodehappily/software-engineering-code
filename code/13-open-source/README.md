<a name="article-16001"></a>

### Article 16001 — License Examination

Public visibility of a repository shall not be interpreted as an unrestricted license to reuse its contents.

<a name="article-16002"></a>

### Article 16002 — Maintainer Availability

A maintainer shall not acquire a twenty-four-hour duty of service by publishing a repository.

<a name="article-16003"></a>

### Article 16003 — Star Evidence

The number of stars shall not constitute conclusive proof of correctness, maintenance, or usefulness.

<a name="article-16004"></a>

### Article 16004 — Fork Sovereignty

A fork may establish an independent jurisdiction subject to the applicable license of its predecessor.

<a name="article-16005"></a>

### Article 16005 — Issue Sufficiency

A report consisting solely of the words it does not work may be returned for further particulars.

<a name="article-16006"></a>

### Article 16006 — Review Time

A pull request shall not acquire priority merely through repeated inquiries about its priority.

<a name="article-16007"></a>

### Article 16007 — Upstream Diplomacy

A downstream modification shall not be attributed to upstream without evidence of its adoption there.

<a name="article-16008"></a>

### Article 16008 — Contributor Recognition

Documentation, tests, and useful bug reports may constitute contributions even when no executable code is added.

<a name="article-16009"></a>

### Article 16009 — Archived Repositories

An archived repository shall not be presumed deceased; it may simply have completed its legislative term.

<a name="article-16010"></a>

### Article 16010 — Release Expectations

An open-source roadmap shall not constitute a delivery contract in the absence of an actual agreement.
