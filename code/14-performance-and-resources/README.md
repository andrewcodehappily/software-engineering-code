<a name="article-17001"></a>

### Article 17001 — Measurement Priority

An optimization shall be required to demonstrate a measurable benefit before claiming victory over readable code.

<a name="article-17002"></a>

### Article 17002 — Complexity Evidence

A favorable asymptotic complexity shall not establish that a program is fast for the input currently before the court.

<a name="article-17003"></a>

### Article 17003 — Benchmark Conditions

A benchmark shall disclose relevant conditions before its results are used to declare another implementation inferior.

<a name="article-17004"></a>

### Article 17004 — Memory Ownership

Memory no longer required by a program shall not be retained solely on the theory that it might become useful someday.

<a name="article-17005"></a>

### Article 17005 — Premature Optimization

The acceleration of a function that has never appeared in a profile shall not automatically qualify as an emergency.

<a name="article-17006"></a>

### Article 17006 — Parallel Proceedings

The multiplication of worker threads shall not be presumed to multiply useful throughput in equal proportion.

<a name="article-17007"></a>

### Article 17007 — Cache Costs

A cache shall be assessed not only by the work it avoids but also by the difficulty of invalidating it.

<a name="article-17008"></a>

### Article 17008 — Latency Distribution

A satisfactory average latency shall not extinguish the complaints of users in the slowest percentile.

<a name="article-17009"></a>

### Article 17009 — Resource Limits

The availability of additional RAM shall not constitute authorization to consume all of it.

<a name="article-17010"></a>

### Article 17010 — Energy Accounting

A program completing its task sooner shall not automatically be deemed more energy efficient without measurement.
