<a name="article-22001"></a>

### Article 22001 — Nature of penalties

Penalties under this fictional part shall consist of documentation, review, tests or other harmless engineering remedies; no actual punishment is authorized.

<a name="article-22002"></a>

### Article 22002 — Unlawful certainty

One who declares a defect fixed without any verification may be sentenced to reproduce the original report.

<a name="article-22003"></a>

### Article 22003 — False green

Disabling a failing test solely to obtain a green badge shall not constitute a lawful acquittal.

<a name="article-22004"></a>

### Article 22004 — Evidence destruction

Deleting a useful diagnostic before recording the relevant facts may require reconstruction of the investigation, subject to legitimate privacy and retention requirements.

<a name="article-22005"></a>

### Article 22005 — Unlicensed certainty

A person imposing a deadline without consulting those performing the work may be ordered to participate in estimation.

<a name="article-22006"></a>

### Article 22006 — Identity confusion

Blaming a contributor solely because their avatar appears beside a commit shall be inadmissible without further evidence.

<a name="article-22007"></a>

### Article 22007 — Unauthorized scope

Bundling an unrelated rewrite into a minor bug fix without disclosure may result in an order to separate the changes.

<a name="article-22008"></a>

### Article 22008 — False documentation

A setup guide knowingly omitting an indispensable dependency may be ordered to stand trial on a clean machine.

<a name="article-22009"></a>

### Article 22009 — Recursive meetings

Scheduling a meeting solely to schedule the next meeting may be remedied by a calendar invitation containing the actual decision.

<a name="article-22010"></a>

### Article 22010 — Attempted refactor

A proposed refactor abandoned after its tests fail shall not be punished merely for having been attempted.

<a name="article-22011"></a>

### Article 22011 — Voluntary disclosure

An engineer promptly reporting their own mistake shall receive credit for enabling timely mitigation.

<a name="article-22012"></a>

### Article 22012 — Rehabilitation

A person completing the necessary corrective work shall not be permanently identified by the title of their old incident.
