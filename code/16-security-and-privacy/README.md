<a name="article-19001"></a>

### Article 19001 — Secret Status

A secret committed to a public repository shall not regain secrecy merely because the commit is later reverted.

<a name="article-19002"></a>

### Article 19002 — Authentication Distinction

Successful authentication shall not constitute permission to perform every available operation.

<a name="article-19003"></a>

### Article 19003 — Minimum Access

A request for read access shall not automatically be enlarged into a grant of administrative authority.

<a name="article-19004"></a>

### Article 19004 — Input Testimony

User input shall not be presumed trustworthy merely because it was submitted through a visually attractive form.

<a name="article-19005"></a>

### Article 19005 — Disclosure Procedure

A vulnerability report shall not require the public disclosure of secrets as a condition of being taken seriously.

<a name="article-19006"></a>

### Article 19006 — Logging Privacy

The wish to debug an incident shall not authorize the collection of every piece of personal information available.

<a name="article-19007"></a>

### Article 19007 — Security Theater

A security control shall be assessed by the protection it provides rather than the severity of its warning colors.

<a name="article-19008"></a>

### Article 19008 — Encryption Claims

The appearance of ciphertext shall not establish that keys and plaintext are appropriately protected.

<a name="article-19009"></a>

### Article 19009 — Patch Priority

A known serious vulnerability shall not be eligible for ordinary non-reproducible bug immunity.

<a name="article-19010"></a>

### Article 19010 — Data Retention

The possibility that data might someday be useful shall not alone justify keeping it indefinitely.
